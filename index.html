8.4

Worked example: TIMS scenario

CASETeachers' Information Management System
▸

ABC College needs a database to manage Teachers, Classes, and Subjects. Key facts from the scenario:

  • Teachers have: EmpNo (key), Name, Address, ContactNo, Date of Appointment, Username, Password, Role.
  • A teacher can teach more than one subject; a subject can have more than one teacher — many-to-many.
  • Each class has a ClassCode (key), Grade, Location, and exactly one teacher-in-charge — one-to-one for that relationship.
  • One teacher assigns subjects to classes.

This is exactly the kind of scenario A/L exam papers use — identify entities first, then attributes, then relationship cardinalities.

3NF walkthrough

EGRemoving transitive dependency from Authors
▸

Author_ID → Author → Author_Nationality is a transitive dependency (Nationality depends on Author, which depends on Author_ID, not directly on the key alongside Book). Split into two tables: Books(Book_ID, Book, Author_ID) and Authors(Author_ID, Author, Author_Nationality). This cuts duplication and protects data integrity.
